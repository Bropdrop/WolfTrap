01 / WHAT CHANGES

A controlled pattern comparison

Alternating patternA B A B A → BChanged counterpart: A C A C A
Token-count controlA A B B AChanged counterpart: A A C C A

Both patterns contain the same token counts and final anchor. The control also changes token positions and distances; it still contains repeated words. Both orientations swap B and C, so their observations are related within each lexical group.

The outcome is logit(target) − logit(alternative). Restoration Δ is the patched changed-input margin minus its unpatched margin. The discovery score is the mean repetition restoration Δ − control restoration Δ, in raw logits. Positive and negative values are retained. This contrast is not a probability, safety score, or a unique diagnosis of an induction circuit.

How to read the result

The experiment can establish a measured contribution from a selected projected head output under these inputs and interventions. It does not identify a complete circuit, establish that the head is necessary or sufficient in general, or prove an induction mechanism. Attention pattern analysis, broader sequence tests, and further interventions would be different evidence.

Selection searches 72 candidates; the separate validation groups help test the selected effect but remain a small related synthetic set. The report supplies no population confidence interval, security certificate, or inference about intentions. Activation-patching conclusions depend on the chosen contrast and intervention.
